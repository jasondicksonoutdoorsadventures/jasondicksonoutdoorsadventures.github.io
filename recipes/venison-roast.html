Venison Roast

Tender wild game seasoned with garlic, rosemary, thyme, and a rich pan sauce.

A venison roast is a simple and flavorful way to enjoy lean wild game. Slow roasting keeps it tender and juicy, while a savory seasoning blend brings out its rich taste.

Ingredients

  • 3–4 lb. venison roast (loin, shoulder, or rump)
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh rosemary, chopped (or 1 tsp dried)
  • 1 tbsp fresh thyme, chopped (or 1 tsp dried)
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1/2 tsp onion powder
  • 1/2 tsp garlic powder
  • 1/2 cup red wine (optional)
  • 1 cup beef or venison stock
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • Fresh parsley, chopped (for garnish)

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 325°F. Pat the venison roast dry with paper towels.
  2. Mix the garlic, rosemary, thyme, salt, pepper, smoked paprika, onion powder, and garlic powder. Rub the mixture evenly over the roast.
  3. Heat the olive oil in a large oven-safe skillet over medium-high heat. Sear the roast on all sides for 2–3 minutes per side until browned.
  4. Add the red wine, if using, and the beef or venison stock. Scrape up the browned bits from the bottom of the pan.
  5. Transfer the skillet to the oven and roast for 45–60 minutes, depending on size, until the internal temperature reaches 130°F for medium-rare or 140°F for medium.
  6. Remove the roast from the oven and let it rest for 10–15 minutes before slicing.
  7. While the roast rests, simmer the pan drippings on the stovetop. Stir in the butter to make a simple pan sauce.
  8. Slice the roast against the grain, spoon the pan sauce over the top, and garnish with fresh parsley.

Serve

Serve with roasted vegetables, potatoes, or your favorite side dish.

Jason's Outdoor Tips

  • Do not overcook venison. It is best served medium-rare to medium.
  • Let the meat rest for 10–15 minutes so it stays juicy.
  • Slice against the grain for the most tender texture.
  • Use an instant-read meat thermometer for the most reliable result.
